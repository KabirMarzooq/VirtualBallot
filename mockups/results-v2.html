What changed, and why

DecisionReasoning
A leaderboard index The current page gives every position an equal-weight card, so with 14 positions you scroll a wall of boxes to answer "who won?". The index is one constant-height row per position — leader plus margin over second — so the whole election reads in a single glance and the answer doesn't degrade as the ballot grows. On desktop it becomes a sticky side column; on mobile it sits above the detail and each row jumps to its section.
Margin, not just totals "+112" is the number that tells you whether a race is decided. A close race (under 5%) is marked in blue, a tie in amber. No other product screen surfaces this.
Top two, then "show all" With 12 candidates per position the tail is noise. Two rows show the contest; the rest are one tap away. Fixes the vertical explosion without hiding anything.
Hierarchy from type, not colour The leader is larger and heavier; everyone else is one flat weight. Blue appears only on the leader's figure and bar. Removes the badge-on-every-row pattern.
Rules instead of nested cards Position → winner-strip → candidate-card was three levels of box. Hairlines and whitespace do the same grouping with far less furniture, and it reads like a results sheet rather than a dashboard.
No horizontal scroll Every row is a 3-column grid that reflows; nothing is a table. At 390px the layout is the same layout, not a squeezed one.

Weak hierarchyAddressed. Three deliberate levels: tallies → leaders → detail.
Inter + Lucide defaultsRetained. These are the project's existing tokens; changing them would break consistency with the other 28 screens. The clichés above are what actually read as AI-generated — the typeface alone does not.
One conflict worth your call. You approved the ribbon background (corner washes, diagonal seam, drifting motes) for Results in the last round. Soft ambient washes behind a results page are close to the "aurora background" tell, and they compete with the thin rules this layout depends on.
